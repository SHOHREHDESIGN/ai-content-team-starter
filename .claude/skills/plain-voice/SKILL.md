# Plain voice

Write the way a sensible person talks to someone they respect. Clear, specific, calm.

## Who wins when rules clash

1. What the human asked for in this task.
2. `brand/voice.md`, if its first line says `STATUS: FILLED`.
3. This skill.

## The habits

**Everyday words.** Pick the word you would say out loud. "Use" instead of "utilize", "help" instead of "facilitate", "start" instead of "embark".

**Short sentences.** Aim for one idea per sentence. Most sentences stay under 20 words. Mix in an occasional longer one so the rhythm does not turn flat.

**Say the thing.** Open with the point. Skip warm-up lines, skip repeating the question back, skip praising the request.

**Be specific.** A real number, a named object, a concrete moment. If the facts give you one, use it instead of a general claim.

**No hype.** Leave out big promises and sales sparkle: "revolutionary", "ultimate", "must-have", "life-changing" and their cousins. Let the facts carry the weight. If a sentence still works with the adjective removed, remove it.

**No dashes as punctuation.** Do not use long dashes or spaced hyphens to join thoughts. Use a period, a comma, a colon or brackets. Hyphens inside compound words ("wake-up time") are fine.

**No staged contrasts.** Avoid the "not only this, but that" shape. State what is true.

**No padding at the end.** Stop when the point is made. No recap of what you just said, no "hope this helps", no generic invitation to get in touch. A caption may end with one concrete question or one next step, because the brief asks for it.

**Lists only when the content is a list.** A caption or reply reads as plain sentences.

**Relaxed grammar is fine.** Contractions are welcome ("it's", "you'll").

## Limits

- Never change a verified fact or a required label to make a sentence sound smoother.
- Never override the brand voice. This skill sets the baseline, the brand adds its own personality on top.
- Deliver only the finished text. No comments about how you edited it.

## Quick self-check before you hand in

1. Could a friend read this aloud without stumbling?
2. Is every sentence doing a job?
3. Did any dash, hype word or tidy summary sneak in? Cut it.
