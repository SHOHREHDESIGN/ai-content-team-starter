# Example facts: Fernhill Sleep Studio (FICTIONAL)

setup. Fernhill Sleep Studio does not exist. Every number below is made up. Replace this

How the team reads this file: each line has an ID (F1, F2, ...). The researcher cites

## The brand

- F1. Fernhill Sleep Studio is a small online studio that teaches a self-paced program
  called "Evening Reset" for adults who struggle to wind down at night.
- F2. Evening Reset is a 14-day program. Each day is one short audio lesson (5 to 8
  minutes) plus one small evening task.
- F3. The program is educational and general-wellness only. It is not medical advice and
  does not treat sleep disorders.
- F4. The audience is adults aged 28 to 45 with desk jobs who say their evenings "disappear
  into the phone".

## Member survey (fictional data, 2025, n = 412 members who finished the program)

- F5. 71% of respondents said falling asleep felt "easier" after the 14 days.
- F6. 64% said the single most useful change was keeping the same wake-up time every day,
  including weekends.
- F7. 23% said the most useful change was an earlier bedtime.
- F8. The median time respondents reported to fall asleep went from 38 minutes to 24
  minutes (self-reported, not measured).

## The wake-up time lesson (Day 4 of Evening Reset)

- F9. The Day 4 lesson explains that a steady wake-up time gives the body one fixed
  anchor, while a "perfect" bedtime changes with how the evening goes.
- F10. The Day 4 task: pick one wake-up time you can keep for 7 days, set one alarm,
  and do not hit snooze.
- F11. Fernhill recommends keeping the wake-up window within 30 minutes on weekends.

## Wording rules for this brand

- F12. Fernhill never promises to "cure" insomnia or guarantees results. It says what
  members reported.
- F13. Survey results are always labeled "self-reported".
- F14. Fernhill does not use emoji in captions.
- F15. Call to action for posts: invite people to try the Day 4 task. No link is needed
  in the caption.
